GS-1 SOCIETY: Population: Demographic Dividend, Ageing and Migration
========================================

Q1. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Examine the imperative of designing an integrated national policy framework that simultaneously leverages India's demographic dividend while proactively preparing for the social and economic implications of an ageing population. Discuss the complexities of balancing these often competing demands on public resources.

Q2. (10 Marks | 150 words)
What do you understand by a 'holistic approach' to population management that extends beyond fertility control? How have evolving patterns of internal and international migration, including reverse migration trends post-pandemic, reshaped India's population challenges and policy priorities?

Q3. (15 Marks | 250 words)
Critically analyse the socio-economic and gendered implications of increasing male out-migration from rural areas on the well-being of left-behind women, children, and the elderly in their villages of origin. Discuss the challenges it poses for local governance, social security mechanisms, and the rural economy, suggesting comprehensive policy interventions.

Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Superintendent of Police Vikram Singh, a diligent and upright IPS officer, currently heads the Vigilance Branch in Rudrapur District. For the past six months, he has been quietly investigating irregularities in the procurement of essential supplies and infrastructure development for police welfare housing, a scheme crucial for boosting constable morale.

His investigation has uncovered a systematic embezzlement of funds, revealing a nexus between certain contractors and senior officials within the State Police Department, including his immediate superior, the Deputy Inspector General (DIG). The evidence is substantial, but exposing it would not only implicate colleagues but also severely tarnish the reputation of the force and potentially lead to a major political scandal involving a local minister known to patronize some of the implicated contractors. The DIG has subtly warned Vikram about the 'fragility of institutional trust' and the 'risks of internal witch-hunts'.

Vikram's decision will directly affect the morale and trust of the constabulary, the public's perception of law enforcement, the careers of implicated officials, and his own professional future. Both exposing the truth and suppressing it carry significant, far-reaching consequences for justice, accountability, and institutional integrity.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Vikram Singh? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
