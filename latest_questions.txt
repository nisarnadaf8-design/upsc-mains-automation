Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY

Ms. Anjali Sharma, a diligent District Collector in the Surajpur district, a region primarily inhabited by the indigenous 'Gond' tribal community, has been overseeing several developmental projects. The state government has recently approved a major industrial corridor project, touted as a game-changer for economic growth and employment generation, slated to pass through a substantial portion of ancestral tribal land.

The Chief Minister's Office has conveyed an urgent directive to Ms. Sharma to expedite land acquisition, bypassing certain environmental and social impact assessments to meet an aggressive project deadline. However, Ms. Sharma's preliminary assessment, supported by local activists and community leaders, indicates that the project, if implemented as per the revised plan, would lead to irreversible displacement of thousands of tribal families, destroy their traditional livelihoods, and desecrate their sacred spiritual sites, potentially violating their constitutional rights. She faces intense pressure from the Chief Secretary to comply swiftly, while tribal representatives are appealing to her for justice and protection of their heritage.

Key stakeholders include the tribal community, the state government, prospective industries, local political leaders, and environmental advocacy groups. A hurried decision could lead to widespread social unrest, ecological damage, and prolonged legal challenges, while non-compliance might result in severe disciplinary action against Ms. Sharma and a significant setback to a project considered vital for state revenue.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Ms. Anjali Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
