Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Dr. Priya Sharma is the District Collector of Sundargarh, a mineral-rich, forest-dense district in a tribal-majority state. She has diligently worked on inclusive development initiatives, earning significant trust from the local communities. The district is now earmarked for a major industrial corridor project, a flagship initiative of the state government aimed at boosting economic growth and attracting investments.

The state government has issued an urgent directive to expedite land acquisition for the industrial corridor, implying a bypass of certain environmental and social impact assessments stipulated under existing tribal land protection laws. A significant portion of the proposed land belongs to Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs) who hold customary forest rights and whose livelihoods are intricately linked to the land and forest produce. The Chief Secretary has personally called Dr. Sharma, emphasizing the project's strategic importance and the need for "zero delays." Meanwhile, tribal community leaders, backed by local activists, have approached Dr. Sharma with petitions and emotional appeals, highlighting the catastrophic displacement and loss of cultural identity if the project proceeds as ordered.

Dr. Sharma's decision will profoundly affect thousands of tribal families, their ancestral lands, and traditional way of life. It also has significant implications for the state's industrial development agenda, investor confidence, and Dr. Sharma's career, as defiance could lead to professional repercussions while compliance could erode public trust and exacerbate social unrest.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Dr. Priya Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
