Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY

Ms. Anya Sharma, a diligent District Collector in the resource-rich but socio-economically underdeveloped district of Rudraprayag, has always prioritised community welfare. Her office has been lauded for its empathetic approach to public grievances. The state government, eager to boost industrialisation and employment, recently signed a Memorandum of Understanding with a major mining corporation for a massive bauxite extraction project in the district.

The project, deemed strategically important by the Chief Secretary, requires the immediate displacement of a cluster of three tribal villages, whose inhabitants have lived in harmony with the forest for generations. While the government order promises a generous rehabilitation package, local tribal leaders and a prominent tribal rights activist have approached Ms. Sharma, presenting compelling evidence that the proposed compensation is inadequate, culturally insensitive, and ignores their customary land rights under existing tribal welfare laws. The Chief Minister's office has conveyed through the local MLA the urgency of expediting land acquisition, citing potential loss of investment if there are delays.

Any swift action to acquire land without due process could spark widespread protests, potentially leading to social unrest and human rights violations, further alienating the vulnerable tribal population. Conversely, delaying the project could invite disciplinary action against Ms. Sharma, jeopardise her career, and be seen as obstructionist by the state leadership, leading to a loss of development opportunity as projected by the government.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Ms. Anya Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
