Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY

Aarav Sharma is the District Collector of a remote, hilly district in a state known for its rich biodiversity and indigenous tribal communities. His current priority is the land acquisition for a critical National Highway expansion project, aimed at improving strategic connectivity and boosting regional economic development. The project, sanctioned by the central government, is under immense pressure for timely completion.

However, the proposed alignment of the highway passes through several ancestral tribal villages and dense forest patches, which are crucial for the livelihood and cultural identity of the local Munda tribe. The community has expressed strong resistance, citing irreversible damage to their sacred groves, displacement, and inadequate rehabilitation plans, garnering significant media attention and support from environmental activists. The Chief Secretary has conveyed the state government’s unequivocal commitment to the project, hinting at severe repercussions for any delay, while the local MLA is actively supporting the tribal community, threatening large-scale protests if their demands are not met.

Aarav finds himself at a crossroads where national strategic imperatives clash with deeply entrenched community rights and environmental concerns. Both swift action to clear the path and delaying tactics to accommodate community demands carry serious consequences for the district, the project, and his own career.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Aarav Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
