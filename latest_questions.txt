Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Ms. Anjali Sharma serves as the District Magistrate of Harsil District, a remote tribal-dominated region in a central Indian state. Her district is earmarked for a large-scale national 'Green Energy Corridor' project involving the construction of high-tension power lines and substations, deemed critical for national energy security and economic growth.

The proposed alignment of the power lines passes directly through a sacred grove (Sarna Sthal) crucial to the spiritual and cultural identity of the indigenous 'Gond' community, which has inhabited the area for centuries. While alternative alignments exist, they would significantly increase project costs and delay timelines. The project director is pushing for the original alignment, citing national priority and cost-effectiveness. The local tribal council, led by an influential elder, is vehemently opposing any disturbance to their sacred site, invoking their constitutional rights to cultural preservation and self-governance. Political leaders from the ruling party are pressurising Ms. Sharma to ensure smooth land acquisition for the project, hinting at potential career repercussions if there are delays.

The Gond community faces the displacement of their cultural heritage and disruption of traditional life. The national project faces delays and cost overruns impacting national energy targets. Ms. Sharma's career and the public's perception of government's commitment to both development and community rights are at stake. Both pushing through the original alignment and halting it have serious consequences.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Ms. Anjali Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
