Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Ms. Priya Singh, a recently appointed Joint Secretary in the Ministry of Rural Development, has been tasked with compiling the national progress report for the 'Gramin Rozgar Awas Yojana', a significant housing-cum-employment welfare scheme. Her preliminary analysis of ground data from several states reveals serious discrepancies and underperformance, indicating that official targets are far from being met and funds might have been misutilised.

Just as Ms. Singh prepares to draft an honest report reflecting these realities, she receives an informal yet firm directive from the Political Advisor to the Minister, subtly suggesting that the report should highlight successes and project an optimistic picture, hinting at potential repercussions for the Ministry's image and future fund allocations if negative data is presented. Simultaneously, local media outlets have begun reporting on the plight of genuine beneficiaries who have not received aid, putting pressure on the Ministry for transparency.

Ms. Singh's decision will have widespread repercussions, affecting not only the beneficiaries of the scheme but also the credibility of government reporting and the allocation of future resources for genuine development. Presenting a falsified report might appease her superiors and protect the Ministry's image in the short term, but risks perpetuating inefficiencies and undermining public trust in governance.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Ms. Priya Singh? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
