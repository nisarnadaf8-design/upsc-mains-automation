Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY

Ms. Ananya Sharma, a diligent Joint Secretary in the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change in New Delhi, is currently overseeing critical preparations for India's position at an upcoming global climate financing summit. Her department holds sensitive internal documents detailing India's negotiation strategies, proposed emission reduction timelines, and confidential financial projections related to seeking international climate adaptation funds.

A prominent environmental advocacy group, known for its transparency campaigns, has filed a comprehensive RTI application demanding full disclosure of all these documents, citing the public's right to know about decisions impacting future generations. While Ms. Sharma believes in transparent governance, her Minister and senior colleagues have vehemently instructed her to deny the request, arguing that releasing such information prematurely would severely compromise India's strategic advantage in complex global negotiations, potentially undermining national interests and economic development goals.

Ms. Sharma finds herself in a difficult position, caught between the legitimate demand for public transparency and the imperative of safeguarding national strategic interests. Her decision will significantly affect several stakeholders.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
The ethical issues include transparency vs. official secrecy, national interest vs. public accountability, duty to obey superiors vs. upholding democratic values, and the potential for eroding public trust if information is withheld, or compromising national strategy if disclosed.

(b) What are the options available to Ms. Ananya Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
Option 1: Deny the RTI citing national interest and strategic secrecy. Merits: Protects negotiating position, follows orders. Demerits: Undermines transparency, fuels public distrust, potential legal challenge.
Option 2: Release partially redacted documents, withholding only highly sensitive strategic details. Merits: Balances transparency and secrecy, shows good faith. Demerits: Might not fully satisfy applicant, still risks some strategic compromise.
Option 3: Seek ministerial approval to release aggregated, non-sensitive information immediately and commit to full disclosure post-summit. Merits: Respects RTI spirit, maintains public confidence, protects current negotiations. Demerits: Requires significant internal persuasion, may be seen as delaying tactics.

(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
I would recommend Ms. Sharma pursue Option 3. She should advocate for releasing general information regarding India's climate policy objectives and commitments, while temporarily redacting specific negotiation tactics and financial projections under Section 8(1)(a) of the RTI Act (information prejudicing national security or strategic interests). Simultaneously, a public statement could be issued committing to full disclosure of all relevant documents once the global summit concludes and India's position is secured.

To handle pressures, Ms. Sharma should present a detailed legal and ethical brief to her Minister and superiors, explaining how a judicious, time-bound partial disclosure can mitigate public backlash and uphold democratic principles without jeopardizing national interest. She should maintain meticulous records of all communications and decisions, demonstrating diligence and integrity. Directly engaging with the advocacy group to explain the rationale for the temporary redaction and future disclosure commitment would also manage external pressure.
