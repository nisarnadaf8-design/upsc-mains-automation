Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Ms. Ananya Sharma is the District Collector of Surajpur, a district characterized by rich mineral deposits and a substantial indigenous tribal population, many of whom reside in scheduled areas. Her administration has been focused on bridging the socio-economic gap through sustainable development initiatives.

Recently, the State Government issued a high-priority directive for expedited land acquisition for a large-scale mining project in a tribal-dominated block, citing national economic interest. However, tribal communities dependent on these forest lands for sustenance and cultural identity have voiced vehement opposition, alleging insufficient free, prior, and informed consent, inadequate rehabilitation plans, and irreversible environmental damage to their sacred groves. Ms. Sharma is facing intense pressure from the Chief Secretary to complete the process swiftly, while tribal elders and human rights activists are besieging her office, appealing to her constitutional duty to protect their rights.

The decision will profoundly impact the livelihoods, culture, and constitutional rights of thousands of tribal individuals, the state's economic development goals, and the district administration's credibility. Both immediate compliance and defiance carry severe socio-political, economic, and personal repercussions.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Ms. Ananya Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
