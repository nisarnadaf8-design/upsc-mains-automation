Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Ms. Aditi Sharma, District Collector of Gondwana district, a Scheduled Area in Chhattisgarh, has consistently focused on improving the livelihood and education of the indigenous tribal communities. Her district is rich in mineral resources, and a large multi-state mining corporation has been pushing for expedited land acquisition for a new project, promising significant state revenue and local employment.

Recently, the State Government issued a directive for immediate possession of a specific tract of forest land, home to a particularly vulnerable tribal group, for the mining project, citing "public interest and economic development." This order bypasses some established legal safeguards for tribal land. Ms. Sharma knows that this displacement would severely disrupt their traditional way of life, access to forest produce, and spiritual sites, potentially leading to irreparable cultural loss and forced migration without adequate rehabilitation. She faces intense pressure from the Chief Secretary's office for prompt compliance, while tribal leaders and local activists are appealing to her for protection, citing constitutional provisions and human rights.

The tribal community, the mining corporation, the state government, local economy, and the future ecological balance of the region are all significant stakeholders. A decision favouring the project could lead to socio-cultural devastation for the tribals and potential unrest, while non-compliance could lead to disciplinary action against Ms. Sharma, stalled development, and strained state-level relations.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Ms. Aditi Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
