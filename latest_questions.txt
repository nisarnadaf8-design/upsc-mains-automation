GS-1 SOCIETY: Education: Access, Equity, Quality and NEP 2020
========================================

Q1. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Examine how the National Education Policy 2020 seeks to bridge the entrenched socio-economic disparities in accessing quality higher education across diverse regions of India. Discuss the extent to which its reformative measures are likely to foster genuine equity rather than merely expand access.

Q2. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Critically examine the persistent challenges posed by the evolving digital divide in ensuring equitable learning outcomes, particularly for marginalized communities, five years into the implementation of NEP 2020. How does this technological gap exacerbate existing inequalities in educational quality and future employability?

Q3. (15 Marks | 250 words)
Discuss the multi-faceted challenges arising from the increasing commercialisation of school education in India, particularly its impact on student dropout rates among vulnerable groups and their prospects for upward social mobility. Critically analyse this phenomenon from social, economic, and gender perspectives, suggesting policy interventions to safeguard inclusive educational opportunities.

Discuss the significance of 'virtue ethics' in fostering an environment where civil servants prioritize public interest over personal gain, even amidst institutional pressures.

Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY

Manoj Kumar, a diligent District Collector in the flood-ravaged Araria district of Bihar, is grappling with the aftermath of unprecedented monsoon rains in October 2026. Thousands are displaced, infrastructure is severely damaged, and immediate relief in the form of food, shelter, and medical supplies is critical. As the nodal officer for disaster relief operations, he oversees the substantial funds released by both central and state governments to address the humanitarian crisis.

Amidst the chaos, Manoj receives a direct instruction from a senior political leader to sanction a significant portion of the relief funds for a 'reconstruction project' in a politically crucial constituency, which is not among the worst-hit areas. Simultaneously, his immediate superior, the Divisional Commissioner, subtly advises him to prioritize allocations to certain contractors known to be 'reliable' and to expedite approvals bypassing some procedural checks, hinting at future career implications if he fails to cooperate. Local NGOs and affected communities, meanwhile, are clamoring for transparent and immediate aid distribution.

Manoj’s decision will profoundly impact thousands of desperate flood victims, the integrity of public service, and his own conscience and career. Both diverting funds and strictly adhering to rules have serious consequences, affecting lives, public trust, and governmental accountability.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Manoj Kumar? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
