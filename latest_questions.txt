Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY

Anjali Sharma, a young and dynamic IAS officer, has recently been posted as the Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM) in Mandgaon District, a rapidly urbanizing peri-urban area experiencing a boom in infrastructure and real estate development. Her responsibilities include overseeing land records, acquisition processes, and granting necessary approvals.

Within weeks of her posting, Anjali observes a troubling pattern: multiple land acquisition projects meant for public-private partnerships are consistently benefiting a specific consortium of private builders. She finds evidence of undervaluation of agricultural land acquired from vulnerable farmers, deliberate manipulation of survey records, and hurried approvals circumventing due process. Her District Collector, Mr. Verma, subtly advises her to prioritize "expeditious development" and avoid "unnecessary complications," hinting at political sensitivities. Simultaneously, the local MLA expresses 'keen interest' in these projects and reminds Anjali of the 'importance of cooperation' for a smooth career path. Farmers from affected villages approach Anjali with grave concerns, describing threats and coercion, but are too fearful to provide formal complaints.

Anjali faces a profound ethical predicament. Her actions, or inactions, will directly impact the lives of countless marginalized farmers, the integrity of governance, and her own nascent career. She is aware that exposing these irregularities could lead to significant backlash, including threats to her personal safety, a difficult transfer, or professional isolation.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Anjali Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
