Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Ms. Ananya Sharma is serving as the District Collector of a coastal district in Odisha, a region frequently battered by natural calamities. Following a devastating super cyclone 'Varun' that recently hit the district, she has been tirelessly leading large-scale disaster relief operations, overseeing the distribution of emergency aid, food, and the setup of temporary shelters. The public expectation for efficient and ethical relief is immense.

During a routine internal audit and based on credible ground intelligence, Ms. Sharma uncovers alarming evidence suggesting that her immediate superior, the Divisional Commissioner Mr. Rajesh Verma, has been systematically siphoning off a significant portion of the allocated relief funds and diverting essential supplies like tarpaulins, medicines, and food grains. These resources are reportedly being channeled to his favored contractors and personal contacts, gravely compromising the integrity and effectiveness of the entire relief effort. Mr. Verma is known for his political connections and an intimidating work style, and he expects Ms. Sharma's unquestioning compliance and subtle cooperation in certain matters. Meanwhile, the vulnerable populations are suffering due to delayed and insufficient aid.

The affected citizens, particularly the poor and marginalized, are directly impacted by the pilferage of funds and supplies, leading to immense hardship and loss of lives. The integrity of the disaster management system, the public trust in the administration, and Ms. Sharma's career and personal safety are significantly at stake. Both reporting and ignoring this corruption have severe, far-reaching consequences.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case.
(b) What are the options available to Ms. Ananya Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits.
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved from various stakeholders?
