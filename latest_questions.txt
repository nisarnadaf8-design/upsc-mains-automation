========================================

Q1. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Examine the subtle yet pervasive ways caste as a social institution continues to influence interpersonal relationships and resource allocation within India's rapidly urbanising professional sectors, despite overt assertions of meritocracy. Discuss how this persistent influence shapes contemporary social stratification beyond traditional village structures.

Q2. (10 Marks | 150 words)
What do you understand by the concept of "digital sanskritisation" or "digital westernisation" in the context of online communities and gig economy aspirations? Critically examine how these phenomena might redefine or reinforce existing patterns of social mobility and structural barriers in Indian society.

Q3. (15 Marks | 250 words)

Discuss how its implementation navigates the complex interplay of inherited social capital, gendered occupational segregation, and the contemporary discourse on merit, providing suitable examples.

Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY

Ms. Anjali Sharma, a newly appointed District Forest Officer (DFO), is responsible for enforcing environmental regulations in a biodiverse region recently designated an Ecologically Sensitive Zone (ESZ). Her district is slated for a major commercial resort project, promoted as a significant employment generator for the local populace.

The powerful local Member of Parliament (MP), Mr. Rajeev Singh, a key proponent of the project, directly pressures Anjali to expedite clearances and overlook certain environmental impact assessment (EIA) requirements, hinting at significant career repercussions if she obstructs the development. Her immediate superior, the Conservator of Forests, advises her to show "flexibility" to avoid political friction. Meanwhile, local communities are divided, with some welcoming the development for job prospects and others fearing irreversible ecological damage.

Ms. Sharma's decision will directly affect the region's fragile ecosystem, the livelihood and well-being of local communities, the integrity of the Forest Department, and her own professional future. Both strict adherence to rules and strategic compromise carry serious and far-reaching consequences.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Ms. Anjali Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
