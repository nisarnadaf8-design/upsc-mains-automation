Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Ms. Anjali Singh, a District Social Welfare Officer in the drought-prone district of Mahipur, is responsible for implementing critical state-sponsored welfare schemes, including a housing subsidy program for marginalized rural families. Her role demands stringent verification of beneficiary eligibility through field reports and official certificates.

Ahead of a by-election, Anjali faces immense pressure from her superior, the District Collector, and a powerful local MLA. They demand she fast-track issuance of eligibility certificates for a list of individuals provided by the MLA, many of whom Anjali’s preliminary review indicates do not meet the criteria. Issuing false certificates would divert funds from genuine beneficiaries. She is explicitly warned of a punitive transfer and inquiry for non-compliance.

Anjali's decision will directly impact genuine needy families, compromise the integrity of the welfare scheme, erode public trust, and deeply affect her own moral conscience and professional career. Both compliance and defiance present significant repercussions.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
The core ethical issues are public trust erosion, conflict of interest, abuse of authority, and compromise of integrity. Anjali faces a dilemma between upholding professional ethics, distributive justice for genuine beneficiaries, and succumbing to political pressure, risking her career for moral principles.

(b) What are the options available to Ms. Anjali Singh? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
1. Comply: Merits- Avoids transfer, short-term political appeasement. Demerits- Undermines justice, illegal, sets bad precedent, harms integrity and public trust.
2. Directly refuse and report: Merits- Upholds rule of law, protects public interest, maintains integrity. Demerits- High risk of retaliation (transfer, inquiry), isolation.
3. Seek written orders/document: Merits- Creates an audit trail, shifts responsibility, buys time for appeal. Demerits- May not be successful, pressure might intensify, still faces personal risk.

(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
I would recommend Ms. Anjali Singh to unequivocally refuse to issue false certificates. She must communicate her inability to comply, citing specific scheme rules and legal provisions, preferably in writing to her superior. This upholds her constitutional duty, integrity, and public trust, ensuring resources reach the deserving.

To handle pressures, she should meticulously document all directives and conversations, especially the threats. She should explore internal grievance mechanisms or anti-corruption channels available to civil servants. Simultaneously, she must prepare herself mentally for potential adverse transfers or inquiries, understanding that upholding ethical conduct is paramount for a civil servant's long-term moral standing and credibility. Engaging with ethical colleagues for support could also be beneficial.
