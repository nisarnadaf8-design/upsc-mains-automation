Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY

Ms. Ananya Sharma, the newly appointed District Magistrate of Sundergarh, Odisha, is overseeing the acquisition of land for a proposed 2 GW solar power park, deemed a project of national strategic importance. The project site encompasses several tribal villages and a dense patch of community forest critical for their sustenance and cultural practices.

The local Adivasi communities, traditionally dependent on the forest and land, are fiercely resisting displacement, citing violations of their constitutional rights under PESA and Forest Rights Act. A powerful state minister is publicly pressuring Ms. Sharma to expedite land acquisition, emphasizing the project's economic benefits and national security implications. Meanwhile, several NGOs have mobilized, threatening widespread protests and legal action if community consent is not genuinely obtained.

Her decision will directly impact the lives and cultural heritage of thousands of tribal villagers, the timely execution of a strategic national project, and the credibility of the district administration. Failure to act decisively could lead to social unrest, legal battles, and project stagnation, while forceful acquisition could ignite a humanitarian crisis and irreversible damage to community trust.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
Ethical issues include conflicting duties (national interest vs. community rights), fairness, justice, accountability, transparency, environmental ethics, and upholding constitutional principles (PESA, FRA) against political pressure and potential human rights violations.

(b) What are the options available to Ms. Ananya Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
1. Proceed with forceful acquisition: Merits - Meets political pressure, speeds project. Demerits - Violates rights, social unrest, legal challenges, ethical compromise.
2. Halt project/insist on full compliance: Merits - Upholds rights, rule of law. Demerits - Project delay, political backlash, economic loss, seen as obstructionist.
3. Initiate rigorous dialogue and impact assessment: Merits - Seeks informed consent, explores alternatives, builds trust, long-term sustainability. Demerits - Time-consuming, requires extensive effort, may not satisfy all stakeholders.
4. Propose alternative site or modified plan: Merits - Protects community, explores win-win. Demerits - Project cost increase, technical feasibility issues, political resistance.

(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
I recommend Ms. Sharma immediately halt forceful acquisition and initiate a transparent, inclusive dialogue with tribal communities, strictly adhering to PESA and FRA. Concurrently, commission an independent socio-environmental impact assessment, exploring project redesign or less impactful alternative sites.

To handle pressures, she must meticulously document all decisions, citing legal frameworks and ground realities to superiors, emphasizing long-term sustainable development. She should clearly communicate legal obligations and risks of non-compliance to political leadership. With community representatives, she must build trust through transparency, seeking support from relevant departments (Forest, Tribal Welfare) and expert panels to mediate and find equitable solutions. Her focus must be on upholding constitutional rights and achieving national goals ethically.
