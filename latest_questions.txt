GS-1 SOCIETY: Poverty, Social Exclusion and Inclusive Growth
========================================

Q1. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Examine how the growing emphasis on multidimensional poverty, beyond mere income metrics, necessitates a fundamental shift in designing and evaluating India's poverty alleviation strategies. Discuss the policy implications of integrating social deprivations and access to basic services into comprehensive poverty frameworks.

Q2. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Critically examine the concept of 'structural social exclusion' as a perpetuator of intergenerational poverty, particularly among Scheduled Tribes and Dalits. How do persistent educational disparities and limited access to formal institutions reinforce this cycle of deprivation, hindering upward social mobility?

Q3. (15 Marks | 250 words)
Critically analyse the role of flagship rural employment guarantee programs, like MGNREGA, in fostering inclusive growth and mitigating economic inequalities in India. Discuss its effectiveness not just in terms of income generation, but also its social, gender, and environmental impacts, citing examples of both successes and systemic challenges.

========================================

Q4. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Explain the significance of Mahatma Gandhi's concept of 'Sarvodaya' in shaping an equitable and people-centric model of public administration. How can civil servants today integrate the principle of welfare for all, including the most marginalized, into their decision-making processes and resource allocation?

Q5. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Ambedkar emphasized constitutional morality over social morality. How should a civil servant navigate situations where deeply entrenched social customs or community expectations conflict with the principles of equality, liberty, and justice enshrined in the Constitution? Discuss with suitable examples.

Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Rajesh Kumar, a diligent and upright IAS officer, serves as the District Magistrate of Shivpur, a rural district known for its complex land records and agrarian disputes. He has a reputation for impartial decision-making and strict adherence to administrative norms, having effectively resolved several contentious land-related issues during his tenure. His younger brother, Suresh, operates a medium-sized agricultural business in the district.

Recently, a long-standing land dispute involving Suresh and a group of marginalized farmers from a neighboring village reached Rajesh's office for adjudication, as it involves revenue records and alleged encroachment. Suresh has personally approached Rajesh, appealing to family loyalty and arguing his innocence, while a local political leader, who is also a distant relative, has discreetly pressured Rajesh to 'expedite a favorable outcome' for his brother. The farmers' collective has also submitted a petition, emphasizing their vulnerability and demanding a fair and transparent resolution.

Rajesh's decision will directly affect his brother's financial standing and reputation, the livelihoods of the marginalized farmers, and the perception of justice in the district. Both a perceived biased action or a strictly impartial one could lead to public distrust, political backlash, or family alienation, gravely impacting his professional integrity and personal life.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Rajesh Kumar? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
