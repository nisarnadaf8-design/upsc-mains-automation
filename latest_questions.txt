Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY

You are Ms. Priyanka Sharma, a young and dynamic District Magistrate of Balia district, known for its rich biodiversity and home to several indigenous communities. Your district has been identified as a strategic location for a crucial national infrastructure project – a high-speed rail corridor connecting major economic hubs, vital for national development and reducing transit times significantly.

The proposed alignment, however, passes directly through a sacred grove central to the spiritual and cultural identity of the indigenous 'Vanvasis' community, who hold traditional rights over the forest land. They are vehemently opposing the project, citing desecration of their ancestral worship site and loss of their traditional way of life. Your superior, the Divisional Commissioner, is pressuring you to expedite land acquisition, emphasizing national priority, while a powerful local politician is instigating the community, demanding a complete rerouting, regardless of the increased costs and delays.

Your decision will profoundly impact the developmental trajectory of the nation, the economic prospects of many, and the cultural survival and rights of the indigenous community. Both proceeding as planned and halting the project carry significant social, economic, and ethical ramifications.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
The primary ethical issues involve balancing national development against indigenous community rights, cultural preservation versus economic progress, and the potential for injustice or exploitation. It also tests the integrity of administrative decision-making under political pressure.

(b) What are the options available to Priyanka Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
1. Proceed with original alignment: Merits include faster project completion, economic benefits, fulfilling superior's directive. Demerits: desecration of sacred site, violation of indigenous rights, social unrest, loss of trust.
2. Recommend full rerouting: Merits: protects community rights, avoids cultural destruction, promotes social harmony. Demerits: significant cost escalation, project delays, political backlash, perceived obstruction of national interest.
3. Propose a modified alignment with dialogue: Merits: attempts to balance development with community rights, explores alternatives, fosters participation. Demerits: requires extensive negotiation, may still not satisfy all parties, potential for compromise on project efficiency.

(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
I would recommend Priyanka pursue Option 3: Propose a modified alignment after extensive, transparent dialogue with the Vanvasis community, project engineers, and relevant experts. This approach prioritizes participatory justice and attempts to find a mutually acceptable solution that minimizes cultural impact while still serving national interest.
To handle pressures: I would formally document all communications and concerns from the community, superior, and politician. I would present a detailed report to the Divisional Commissioner outlining the social and ethical risks of proceeding without community consent, emphasizing the long-term stability benefits of a more inclusive approach. I would also leverage the project's Public Relations team to explain the complexities and options to a wider audience, thereby countering political manipulation with facts and transparency, aiming for a resolution that respects both development and human dignity.
