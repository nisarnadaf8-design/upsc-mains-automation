========================================

Q1. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Examine how the persistent structural inequalities rooted in caste, class, and gender critically undermine the effective delivery and equitable access to public health services, particularly for vulnerable populations in remote and peri-urban areas.

Q2. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Critically examine the extent to which India's current public health financing models adequately address the complex interplay between chronic non-communicable diseases, mental health challenges, and the rising out-of-pocket expenditure, especially in the context of an aging population.

Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Alok Sharma, a diligent District Environmental Officer with the State Pollution Control Board in Bharatpur, has recently taken charge. Known for his principled stance, he is eager to ensure compliance with environmental norms in this district, which is experiencing significant industrial growth and hosts several large manufacturing units. His initial reports indicate increasing pollution levels in local water bodies.

During a routine inspection, Alok's team discovers serious violations by "IndusCo Pvt. Ltd.," a major industrial player and the largest employer in the region, related to the untreated discharge of industrial effluents into the nearby river, affecting downstream agricultural lands and villages. Before Alok can initiate legal action, his immediate superior, the Director, subtly advises him to adopt a "pragmatic approach" given IndusCo's economic footprint. Concurrently, a powerful local MLA contacts Alok, emphasizing the company's contribution to employment and warning against any action that could "disrupt development," hinting at potential career hurdles.

Alok faces immense pressure as local villagers and environmental activists are preparing for protests, citing severe health issues and agricultural losses. His decision will significantly impact the health and livelihoods of thousands, the reputation of the State Pollution Control Board, the local economy, and his own professional integrity.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Alok Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
