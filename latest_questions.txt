Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Ms. Ananya Sharma, a newly posted Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM) in the coastal district of Sagarika, is leading a critical relief operation following a devastating cyclone. Her primary responsibility is to ensure the timely and transparent distribution of emergency aid, essential supplies, and rehabilitation funds to thousands of displaced families.

While reviewing ground reports and audit trails for a major relief fund, Ananya stumbles upon irrefutable evidence indicating a well-orchestrated scheme of embezzlement of essential goods and funds. The trail leads directly to Mr. Rajesh Verma, the District Collector, who is widely known for his political patronage and authoritarian style. Ananya faces immense pressure: exposing her superior could mean severe professional repercussions and personal targeting, yet remaining silent would betray her duty to the suffering populace and perpetuate a grave injustice.

The primary stakeholders are the vulnerable cyclone victims, whose survival depends on the aid. Her decision will significantly impact their welfare, the morale of honest officials, the public's trust in governance, and the overall integrity of the disaster management framework. Both reporting the corruption and choosing to overlook it carry profound consequences for all involved.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Ms. Ananya Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
