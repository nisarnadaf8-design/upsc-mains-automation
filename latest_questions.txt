Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Ms. Ananya Sharma is an Under Secretary in the Ministry of Defence (MoD), New Delhi, specifically handling procurement records. She is responsible for maintaining sensitive project files related to a recently awarded defence contract for critical aerospace components. This contract involves a foreign vendor and has been a subject of public debate regarding its value and specifications in national media.

An RTI applicant, a prominent investigative journalist, has requested detailed financial clauses, technical specifications, and internal appraisal reports related to this specific defence contract. Ms. Sharma knows that while public interest demands transparency, revealing certain details could compromise national security, expose sensitive technological know-how of a strategic partner, and potentially lead to competitive disadvantages for future indigenous development. Her Joint Secretary has explicitly cautioned her about maintaining secrecy, citing the Official Secrets Act. Simultaneously, a leading newspaper has already published speculative articles hinting at alleged irregularities, mounting public pressure for full disclosure.

Her decision will directly affect the applicant's right to information, national security interests, the reputation of the MoD, the foreign vendor's trust, and potentially the integrity of future defence acquisitions. Both full disclosure and complete denial carry significant and potentially damaging consequences.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
The core ethical issues are the conflict between public's right to information (transparency) and national security (official secrecy). It also involves upholding integrity and accountability in public administration, managing public trust, and preventing misinformation versus protecting strategic defence interests and international relations.

(b) What are the options available to Ms. Ananya Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
1. Disclose full information: Merits – Upholds transparency, addresses public concerns, reinforces RTI spirit. Demerits – Severely compromises national security, violates Official Secrets Act, strains diplomatic relations with strategic partners.
2. Deny all information citing national security: Merits – Protects sensitive data, adheres to superior’s directive. Demerits – Erodes public trust, fuels suspicion of wrongdoing, appears opaque, disregards the spirit of RTI.
3. Partial disclosure with careful redaction: Merits – Balances transparency with security, demonstrates accountability, mitigates public distrust. Demerits – Risks accusations of selective disclosure, might not fully satisfy the applicant, requires meticulous justification.

(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
I would recommend Ms. Sharma opt for partial disclosure through careful redaction of information genuinely compromising national security or strategic interests, while releasing all other non-sensitive contractual details. This approach best upholds both transparency and national interest, demonstrating responsible governance and accountability without endangering state security.
To handle pressures: I would thoroughly document the rationale for redactions, citing specific sections of relevant acts (RTI Act 2005 exemptions, Official Secrets Act). I would proactively brief my superior, the Joint Secretary, explaining the legal basis and ethical considerations for this balanced approach. A transparent press note from the MoD explaining the rationale for redacted information would also manage public expectations and scrutiny.
