GS-1 SOCIETY: Poverty, Social Exclusion and Inclusive Growth
========================================

Q1. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Examine how the evolving understanding of multidimensional poverty challenges the traditional 'trickle-down' approach to economic development in India. Discuss the imperative for policy shifts towards more targeted and comprehensive inclusive growth strategies that address non-income deprivations.

Q2. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Critically examine the persistent intergenerational social immobility faced by certain historically marginalized communities in India, despite targeted welfare interventions. How does this cycle relate to educational deprivation, lack of access to quality healthcare, and systemic exclusion from economic opportunities?

Q3. (15 Marks | 250 words)
Discuss how intersecting vulnerabilities — social, economic, and political — perpetuate the exclusion of Scheduled Tribes and other minority groups from the benefits of India's rapid economic growth narrative. Analyse the policy and institutional reforms required to foster genuine inclusive growth for these communities, moving beyond mere welfare provision.

Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY

Manoj Kumar, an upright and highly respected District Collector of Aridpur District, is overseeing a critical land acquisition process for the national express highway project. Aridpur is known for its complex land records and significant socio-economic disparities, making this project particularly sensitive. Manoj has a reputation for meticulous adherence to rules and public welfare, and his department is under scrutiny to ensure fair and transparent proceedings.

During the final verification phase, Manoj discovers that a substantial parcel of land, recently purchased by a proxy for his estranged elder brother, Akash Kumar, falls directly within the high-compensation zone of the project. Akash, known for his speculative land dealings, subtly contacts Manoj, appealing to family ties and expecting favorable valuation beyond standard procedures. Simultaneously, a powerful local MLA, who is close to Akash, hints at the importance of "expediting" this particular land parcel's compensation without undue scrutiny.

The primary stakeholders include the genuinely displaced farmers, the state government (representing public interest and funds), the national express highway project, Manoj's own integrity, and his family. A wrong decision could lead to allegations of corruption, public outcry, jeopardizing the project, or compromising Manoj's ethical standing and the trust placed in his office.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Manoj Kumar? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved from his brother and the local MLA? (150 words)
