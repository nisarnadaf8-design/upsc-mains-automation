Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Ms. Meera Sharma, a relatively new Block Development Officer (BDO) in the Nandanpur Block of a drought-prone district, is known for her diligence and adherence to rules. She is currently overseeing the implementation of a crucial government welfare scheme designed to provide immediate financial relief and agricultural subsidies to genuinely distressed small and marginal farmers, requiring rigorous verification of land records.

With local elections approaching, the influential local MLA approaches Meera, strongly recommending the expedited issuance of land-ownership certificates for approximately fifty individuals who are known party loyalists but do not meet the scheme's strict eligibility criteria. The MLA assures her a smooth and rewarding tenure if she complies, hinting at severe repercussions, including a punitive transfer or departmental inquiry, if she obstructs their 'party's developmental agenda.' Her immediate superior, the District Collector, has also subtly conveyed the importance of 'cooperating with local elected representatives for harmonious functioning.'

Meera faces an acute ethical dilemma. Issuing false certificates would deprive genuine, vulnerable farmers of critical aid and undermine public trust in governance, while refusing could jeopardize her career and potentially expose her to harassment, affecting her family's stability.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Meera Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
