Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Aarav Sharma, a newly appointed District Magistrate in the industrially developing Rampur district, was known for his proactive approach to social welfare, particularly child rights. On 10th November 2026, during a routine but unannounced inspection of a manufacturing cluster in a less regulated zone, he aimed to assess working conditions.

To his dismay, Aarav discovered multiple child labourers, some as young as nine, working in hazardous conditions in a prominent garment factory. Further investigation revealed the factory was beneficially owned by none other than Minister Raghavendra Singh, a highly influential state cabinet minister with significant political clout. Aarav's immediate superior, the Divisional Commissioner, subtly cautioned him, emphasizing 'political sensitivities' and the upcoming state elections, suggesting a more 'pragmatic' and less confrontational resolution.

Aarav now faces immense pressure to either dilute his findings or overlook the transgression. His decision will directly impact the exploited children, the Minister's political standing, the factory's operations, the credibility of the district administration, and potentially Aarav's own career trajectory. Both decisive action and passive inaction carry severe and far-reaching consequences.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Aarav Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
