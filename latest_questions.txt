GS-1 SOCIETY: Health: Public Health System and Social Determinants
========================================

Q1. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Examine the intricate relationship between persistent social inequities and the effectiveness of India's public health system in building resilience against recurrent and emerging health challenges. Discuss how addressing these determinants is critical for sustainable health security.

Q2. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Critically examine the extent to which the Ayushman Bharat scheme, through its two pillars, has truly democratized healthcare access and reduced out-of-pocket expenditure, especially for marginalized communities, in the context of persistent infrastructural and human resource gaps.

Q3. (15 Marks | 250 words)
Critically analyse how the persistent challenges of malnutrition, particularly stunting, wasting, and anaemia, are deeply rooted in a complex interplay of socio-cultural practices, economic disparities, and governance effectiveness across India. Discuss multi-pronged strategies, incorporating gender-sensitive and community-led approaches, essential for achieving sustainable nutritional security and holistic well-being.

Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Ms. Kavita Sharma serves as the District Environmental Officer in Rajnagar, a rapidly industrializing district known for its delicate wetland ecosystems. Having joined the State Pollution Control Board recently, she has quickly established a reputation for diligence and strict adherence to environmental regulations. Her current responsibility involves overseeing compliance of industrial units with pollution norms.

During a routine inspection of Vardhaman Cements Ltd., a prominent cement manufacturer and a significant employer in the district, her team uncovers substantial evidence of untreated industrial effluent discharge into a local river that feeds into the protected wetlands. The plant’s management, citing expansion pressures and critical economic contributions, approaches her with an offer to "resolve" the issue quietly. Soon after, her superior, the Chairperson of the SPCB, advises her to adopt a "flexible approach" given the plant's political backing and importance to the state's economy, subtly hinting at potential negative impacts on her career if she escalates the matter. Simultaneously, local farmers and environmental groups present her with strong community grievances and photographic evidence of ecological damage and health issues.

Kavita faces a profound ethical dilemma. Her decision will directly impact the environmental health of Rajnagar, the livelihoods and well-being of thousands of local residents, the economic stability of a major industry, the credibility of the regulatory body, and her personal and professional integrity. Both immediate action and inaction carry significant and far-reaching consequences.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Ms. Kavita Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
