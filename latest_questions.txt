Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY

Ms. Aditi Sharma, an accomplished IAS officer, currently serves as the District Election Officer (DEO) for Rudrapur District in Uttarakhand. With the impending general elections, her primary responsibility involves the meticulous preparation of accurate electoral rolls and the legitimate issuance of various official certificates essential for candidate eligibility. She is widely respected for her uprightness and commitment to due process.

A few weeks before the nomination deadline, Ms. Sharma receives an implicit instruction, conveyed through her immediate superior, to prioritize and issue domicile certificates for a group of individuals whose residential claims are highly dubious. These individuals are reportedly influential political workers of the incumbent party, needing these specific certificates to qualify for contesting from constituencies where their genuine domicile is questionable. Her superior subtly communicated that compliance would ensure smooth career progression, while non-compliance could lead to administrative hurdles.

Ms. Sharma is now facing a significant ethical dilemma. Upholding electoral integrity and the rule of law demands a thorough verification process, which might disqualify these candidates. However, refusing the directive risks antagonizing powerful political entities, potentially leading to professional retaliation or undeserved transfers. Conversely, issuing false certificates would compromise her personal integrity, violate electoral norms, and undermine public faith in the democratic process.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
The core ethical issues are upholding electoral integrity, impartiality, and the rule of law against political pressure. It involves potential abuse of power, conflict of interest, compromise of a civil servant's honesty and dedication to public service, and the erosion of public trust in democratic institutions.

(b) What are the options available to Ms. Aditi Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
1. Comply and issue certificates:
   Merits: Avoid immediate political confrontation, potentially secure career.
   Demerits: Violates law, personal integrity, undermines elections, potential legal action, loss of public trust.

2. Outright refusal:
   Merits: Upholds law, integrity, strengthens democratic process.
   Demerits: Faces political wrath, career stagnation, potential harassment/transfer, isolation.

3. Document process, seek clarification/guidance from ECI:
   Merits: Follows due process, seeks institutional protection, upholds integrity, creates accountability trail.
   Demerits: May delay process, potentially escalate conflict, might not guarantee full protection.

(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
I would recommend Ms. Sharma adopt a course of action combining option 3 with meticulous documentation. She should politely but firmly communicate her inability to bypass established legal procedures for issuing domicile certificates, citing relevant election rules and statutes. Simultaneously, she must document all communications, formal and informal, related to the pressure. She should then seek urgent guidance from the Election Commission of India (ECI) through official channels, highlighting the potential violation of electoral norms without naming individuals directly initially, focusing on process integrity.

To handle pressures, she should maintain a professional demeanour, avoid emotional reactions, and consistently refer to rules and regulations. By involving the ECI early, she creates an institutional shield against political retribution. Building a factual, documented case would provide her with a strong defence against any attempts to victimize her professionally, reinforcing transparency and accountability.
