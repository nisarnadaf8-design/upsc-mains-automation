GS-1 SOCIETY: Tribal Communities: Issues, Rights and Constitutional Safeguards
========================================

Q1. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Examine how the spirit of constitutional safeguards for tribal communities, beyond the explicit provisions of the Fifth and Sixth Schedules, navigates the complexities of contemporary development paradigms and tribal self-determination movements. Discuss its relevance in ensuring inclusive governance amidst diverse regional aspirations.

Q2. (10 Marks | 150 words)
What do you understand by 'cultural resilience' in the context of Indian tribal communities? How have state-led development initiatives and market forces inadvertently challenged or strengthened this resilience, impacting their unique identities and traditional governance structures over time?

Q3. (15 Marks | 250 words)
Critically analyse the paradox where well-intentioned development projects, while aiming for national progress, often exacerbate socio-economic vulnerabilities and ecological degradation for tribal communities. Discuss how this dynamic fuels local discontent, sometimes feeding into security challenges, and suggest comprehensive legal and administrative reforms needed for equitable resource governance and conflict resolution.

GS-4 ETHICS: Ethics in Public and Private Relationships
========================================

Q4. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Explain the ethical imperative of maintaining objectivity in administrative decision-making when personal relationships or affiliations invariably intersect with professional duties. How does a lack of integrity in such situations erode public trust and undermine good governance in the long run?

Q5. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Examine the ethical challenges faced by public officials in upholding confidentiality and preventing the misuse of privileged information, particularly when personal loyalty or kinship ties exert pressure. What mechanisms can strengthen ethical conduct in ensuring the sanctity of public data and administrative trust?

Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY
Mr. Alok Kumar, a diligent Divisional Forest Officer posted in the mineral-rich Sundargarh district, recently unearthed a complex nexus of financial irregularities amounting to several crores in the procurement of forestry equipment and execution of compensatory afforestation projects. His detailed report, backed by irrefutable evidence, exposed kickbacks and ghost beneficiaries involving influential contractors and some departmental officials.

Upon submitting his findings to the State Vigilance Commission and his immediate superior, the Principal Chief Conservator of Forests (PCCF), Alok began facing subtle yet persistent retaliation. The PCCF subtly advised him to withdraw the complaint, stressing the potential damage to the department's reputation and the 'political sensitivities' involved. Simultaneously, a powerful local MLA, whose relatives were implicated, used intermediaries to hint at punitive transfers and career stagnation for Alok, while his family expressed deep concerns about his safety and future prospects.

Alok's decision will significantly impact his personal integrity and career, the financial health and credibility of the Forest Department, the public's trust in administrative honesty, and the morale of other upright officers. Both pursuing the truth and backing down carry severe, far-reaching consequences for him and the larger administrative ecosystem.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Mr. Alok Kumar? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
