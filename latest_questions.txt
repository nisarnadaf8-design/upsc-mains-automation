Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY

Aarti Sharma, a dedicated and newly appointed District Environment Officer in the State Pollution Control Board, has been recently posted to Ramanagar, a rapidly industrialising district. Her department is responsible for ensuring industries adhere to environmental norms, and Ramanagar's economic growth largely hinges on its burgeoning industrial clusters.

Just weeks into her tenure, a confidential report, corroborated by local media, reveals that "GreenTech Solutions," a prominent manufacturing plant and the largest employer in the district, has been consistently discharging untreated toxic waste into the adjacent river, a major source of water for agricultural communities downstream. Her immediate superior, the Regional Director, subtly advises Aarti to handle the matter "pragmatically," emphasizing GreenTech’s contribution to local employment and the state's investment climate. Simultaneously, the influential local MLA publicly praises GreenTech Solutions and privately warns Aarti against actions that might "disrupt progress." Meanwhile, several farmer collectives are staging protests, threatening legal action and demanding immediate intervention.

Aarti's decision will directly affect her career trajectory, the district's economic stability, the environmental health of the region, and the livelihood of thousands of farmers and villagers dependent on the river. Both immediate punitive action and delayed compliance have severe and far-reaching consequences.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Aarti Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
