Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY

Meena Sharma, a diligent and upright IAS officer, serves as the District Magistrate of Rudrapur, a district known for its rapidly expanding industrial sector. Her administration is focused on promoting industrial growth while ensuring social welfare and strict adherence to labour laws.

During a routine inspection by her labour department team, severe instances of child labour are discovered in a prominent textile factory, 'Rudrapur Looms'. This factory is owned by Mr. Rajendra Pratap, a powerful state cabinet minister and a key political figure in the region. Meena is immediately contacted by the Minister's office, subtly advising her to downplay the report due to its potential "unnecessary political ramifications" and impact on "industrial investment sentiments." Her immediate superior, the Divisional Commissioner, also hints at the sensitivity of the matter, urging caution.

The children involved, their families, the factory's other employees, the Minister's political career, and the integrity of the district administration are all at stake. Both strict action and a lenient approach carry significant consequences for justice, public trust, and Meena's own career.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
The case involves child labour (violation of fundamental human rights), political interference, conflict of interest, integrity versus hierarchical obedience, rule of law, and the maintenance of public trust in administration. Meena faces a critical dilemma between upholding justice and succumbing to pressure.

(b) What are the options available to Meena Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
1. Initiate full legal action against the factory: Merits – Upholds law, protects vulnerable children, reinforces administrative integrity. Demerits – Faces intense political backlash, potential career repercussions, social unrest.
2. Order minimal penalties/warnings without full closure: Merits – Avoids immediate confrontation, may preserve political goodwill. Demerits – Compromises justice, encourages impunity, erodes public trust and her own moral authority.
3. Seek an internal inquiry with strategic delays: Merits – Buys time, allows for discreet information gathering. Demerits – Could be perceived as stalling, risks evidence tampering, might be seen as lacking resolve.
4. Report the matter confidentially to higher central authorities: Merits – Shares responsibility, seeks institutional support. Demerits – May be seen as insubordination, support is not guaranteed, risks being sidelined.

(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
I would recommend initiating full legal action against 'Rudrapur Looms' for severe child labour violations, ensuring immediate rescue and rehabilitation for the affected children. This course of action is paramount to uphold the rule of law, protect vulnerable populations, and maintain the integrity and credibility of the civil services.

To handle the immense pressures, I would:
1. Meticulously document all evidence of child labour and all communications regarding political interference, creating an irrefutable case.
2. Formally inform the Chief Secretary and relevant child protection commissions about the situation and the pressures, seeking their guidance and institutional support to ensure transparency and accountability.
3. Collaborate with NGOs and legal aid bodies to ensure effective rehabilitation for the children and support for their families.
4. Prepare for potential transfers or other retaliatory measures, steadfastly defending my actions based on legal principles and ethical governance, while maintaining a clear and factual public stance if necessary.
