GS-1 SOCIETY: Globalisation and Its Impact on Indian Society
========================================

Q1. (10 Marks | 150 words)
Examine how globalisation has fostered a 'glocal' cultural synthesis in India, simultaneously promoting local expressions while integrating global influences. Discuss the potential challenges this hybridisation poses to the preservation of indigenous cultural distinctiveness in the long run.

Q2. (10 Marks | 150 words)
How has the burgeoning gig economy, a significant outcome of globalised economic trends, reshaped traditional notions of social security and labour solidarity among urban Indian youth? Critically examine its implications for long-term career stability and intergenerational upward mobility.

Q3. (15 Marks | 250 words)
Critically analyse the multi-faceted impact of globalisation on the traditional Indian family structure, particularly highlighting the rise of nuclear families and individualistic aspirations. Discuss how these shifts have profoundly altered caregiving responsibilities for the elderly and redefined women's roles within both the household and the evolving economic landscape.

Q6. (20 Marks | 300 words) — CASE STUDY

Ms. Anjali Sharma, District Project Coordinator for the Department of Rural Development in Sagar district, Madhya Pradesh, is in the final stages of compiling the annual impact report for the 'Gram Samridhhi Yojana'. This flagship welfare scheme aims to uplift marginalized farmers through direct benefit transfers and agricultural support. Her preliminary internal analysis, based on ground surveys and financial records, indicates significant underperformance in several blocks regarding fund utilization and beneficiary outreach.

Her immediate superior, the District Collector, Mr. Ramesh Kumar, has subtly advised her to "adjust the figures" to present a more favourable picture, citing potential negative repercussions for the district's reputation and funding continuity if the report is submitted as is. Simultaneously, the local Member of Legislative Assembly (MLA) has directly contacted Anjali, emphasising the political sensitivity of the report ahead of upcoming elections and suggesting that a positive outcome is crucial for 'public morale'. Anjali knows manipulating data would artificially inflate the scheme's success, masking the ground realities.

Anjali's decision will directly impact thousands of marginalized farmers who genuinely need improved scheme implementation, the credibility of government welfare programs, and the public trust in administrative reporting. Submitting a manipulated report could lead to misinformed policy decisions and continued deprivation for the needy, while an honest report might invite political backlash and jeopardize her professional standing.

(a) Identify and discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (50 words)
(b) What are the options available to Ms. Anjali Sharma? Evaluate each option with its merits and demerits. (100 words)
(c) What course of action would you recommend and why? How would you handle the pressures involved? (150 words)
